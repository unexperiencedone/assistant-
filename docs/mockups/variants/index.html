A · Manga panels

The roll's assignment

Nova speaks in speech balloons and its state is SFX lettering. Ink frames on paper, one pink spot ink for the next action. It's your portfolio's own visual language, so Nova and your portfolio become one personal identity.

Strongest at
Talking with it: a conversation reads like a page you want to read.
Honest risk
A bright paper panel over a dark editor is loud; it may need a softer state for quick answers. Shares its look with the portfolio, which is a pro or a con depending on how separate you want them.

B · Game HUD

My pick

Nova draws a target-lock around exactly what it's reading, answers in an RPG dialogue box, and ends every answer with numbered choices you can just say (“one”). Needs you is a quest tracker; keys 1–4 switch spaces; a timeline of today runs along the bottom.

Strongest at
Seeing what Nova is doing and deciding fast, without leaving the app you're in. The lock makes “help me with what's on the screen” visible.
Honest risk
It's the most familiar of the four: HUD language is everywhere in games, so it's easy to read and less distinctive.

C · Wayfinding

Challenger

Indian-airport signage: bilingual yellow bands, black type, white pictograms. Over an app, Nova drops in as one sign band with a “You are here” marker on your code. The canvas shows only the next decision at monumental scale, then a departures board of everything lined up.

Strongest at
Deciding fast. You can read the next decision and its time from across the room.
Honest risk
Saturated yellow is a lot for an all-day tool, and the conversation is the weakest part here.

D · Living Ink, refined

Your current look, polished

The direction you liked, pushed further: a quiet ink capsule rises over the app with a violet lens on what Nova reads; the canvas lets the conversation lead, the blob lives in the composer, today runs as a ribbon, and Needs you shows a real preview of the post.

Strongest at
